The letters come first. Annie Jump Cannon set the order O, B, A, F, G, K, M by surface temperature at Harvard, starting in 1901. The International Astronomical Union adopted her system in 1922. That story belongs with [The Harvard Computers](../51-harvard-computers/).

The memory line "Oh Be A Fine Girl, Kiss Me" is traditionally credited to Henry Norris Russell, the Princeton astronomer of Hertzsprung–Russell diagram fame. It is said he made it up for his students, probably in the 1910s or 1920s. No dated first printing turns up, and even an astronomy department page at University College London has called the Russell credit "astronomical myth." Treat it as tradition, not as a documented first use. The mnemonic can only be from after Cannon's order existed.

It took off in American college astronomy classes. Later came rewrites, such as "Fine Guy" or "Fine Gal." Harvard's Owen Gingerich ran an annual contest for better versions and wrote about it in 1991. In 1995 the American Astronomical Society's committee on women in astronomy held a contest too, and Russell's line, with "Guy" or "Gal" swapped in, still came out the favorite.

Three cooler classes were added later for brown dwarfs: L and T in 1999, and Y in 2011. A later extension of the line is "long time, y'all," for L, T, and Y.

## Key facts
- Cannon set the temperature order O B A F G K M at Harvard starting in 1901; the IAU adopted it in 1922.
- "Oh Be A Fine Girl, Kiss Me" is traditionally credited to Henry Norris Russell, but no dated first printing is known.
- L and T were added in 1999, and Y in 2011, for brown dwarfs.
