## A ball of thread
**Clue** comes from ***clew***, an old English word for a **ball of thread or yarn**. The story behind it is Greek myth.

## Theseus, Ariadne and the Minotaur
King **Minos** of Crete kept the **Minotaur**, half man, half bull, in the **Labyrinth**, a maze built by Daedalus. Athens had to send young men and women every few years to be fed to it. The hero **Theseus** went as one of them. Minos's daughter **Ariadne** fell in love with him and gave him a **ball of thread**. He tied one end at the entrance, unwound it as he went in, killed the Minotaur, and followed the thread back out.

So a *clew* was the thing that leads you out of a maze, and by the 1600s, spelled **clue**, it meant anything that leads you to the answer to a puzzle.

A **clew** is also a sailing term: the **lower corner of a sail**, where the sheet line attaches.

## Labyrinth: house of the double axe?
**Labyrinth** may come from ***labrys***, the Minoan **double-headed axe**, a sacred symbol carved all over the great palace at **Knossos** on Crete. "Labyrinth" would then mean "**house of the double axe**," the vast, confusing palace itself. The link is likely but not proven.

## Minotaur
*Minotaur* = **Minos** + ***tauros***, **bull**: "Minos's bull." The same *taur* is in **Taurus**, the bull of the zodiac.

## Key facts
- Clue: from clew, a ball of thread, Ariadne's thread for Theseus in the Labyrinth
- The spelling "clue" was in use by the 1600s; a clew is also a sail's lower corner
- Labyrinth may come from labrys, the Minoan double axe: house of the double axe at Knossos
- Minotaur: Minos + tauros, bull, as in Taurus
