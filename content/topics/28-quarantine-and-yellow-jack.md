## Forty days
**Quarantine** comes from Venetian ***quaranta giorni***, "forty days," the time a ship arriving from a plague port had to wait at anchor before anyone could come ashore.

## Ragusa came first, with thirty
The first such law was passed in **1377** by **Ragusa** (today **Dubrovnik**, Croatia), a trading republic on the Adriatic. It required **30 days** of isolation, a *trentino*. **Venice** later stretched it to **40**.

Why forty? The number was more **biblical than medical**: Jesus's forty days in the desert, Noah's forty days of rain, the forty days of Lent, Moses's forty days on the mountain. It just happened to be longer than the plague's incubation period, so it worked.

## The lazaretto
Venice isolated the sick and suspect on an island hospital, the **lazaretto**, named after **Lazarus**: the beggar covered in sores in Jesus's parable (and associated with Lazarus raised from the dead). *Lazar* became an old word for a leper or diseased beggar.

## Yellow Jack
**Yellow Jack** was sailors' slang for **yellow fever**, named for the **jaundice**, the yellowing of the skin and eyes as the disease attacks the liver. A **jack** is a small flag flown at the bow of a ship, so "yellow jack" was both the disease and the flag.

Yellow fever struck the Americas over and over. In **Philadelphia in 1793**, then the US capital, an epidemic killed about **a tenth of the city's people**, and the government fled.

## The Q flag today
Today the plain yellow signal flag stands for the letter **Q** in the international code. Flown alone by an arriving ship, it means the opposite of the old warning: "**my ship is healthy, and I request free pratique**," clearance to come ashore.

One correction to a popular story: the ships under Ragusa's 1377 law did **not** fly a yellow flag. The yellow quarantine flag came much later.

## Key facts
- Quarantine: Venetian quaranta giorni, 40 days
- Ragusa (Dubrovnik) passed the first law in 1377, for 30 days; Venice stretched it to 40
- Forty was biblical (desert, Noah, Lent), but longer than plague's incubation
- The lazaretto was named after Lazarus
- Yellow Jack: sailors' slang for yellow fever, named for jaundice; a jack is a small bow flag
- Philadelphia 1793 lost about a tenth of its people to yellow fever
- The yellow Q flag today means "healthy, requesting clearance"
