## The eruption
**Mount Tambora**, on the island of Sumbawa in Indonesia, erupted in **April 1815**, peaking on April 10–11. It is the **largest eruption in recorded history**. Tens of thousands of people died on the islands from the blast, ash and the famine that followed.

## 1816, the Year Without a Summer
Tambora threw so much sulfur gas into the upper atmosphere that it formed a haze that reflected sunlight around the world. Temperatures fell. In **1816**:
- **Snow fell in June in New England**, and frosts killed crops through the summer. New Englanders called it "eighteen hundred and froze to death."
- Harvests failed across Europe; there was **famine**, food riots and disease.
- Nobody then knew the cause; the link to Tambora was made long afterward.

It's a disaster that reached around the world: a "bad star" in the oldest sense. See [Disaster means bad star](../01-disaster-bad-star/).

## The Villa Diodati
In June 1816 **Lord Byron** rented the **Villa Diodati** on **Lake Geneva** in Switzerland. Nearby stayed **Percy Bysshe Shelley**, **Mary Godwin** (soon to be Mary Shelley), her stepsister Claire Clairmont, and Byron's doctor, **John Polidori**. The cold, stormy weather kept them indoors. Byron challenged everyone to write a **ghost story**.

- **Mary Shelley**, only **18**, after a waking nightmare of a scientist kneeling beside the thing he had put together, began ***Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus***, published in **1818**. Prometheus stole fire from the gods for mankind and was punished forever: the scientist who seizes the power of creation.
- **Polidori** wrote "**The Vampyre**" (1819), the first modern vampire story in English, ancestor of *Dracula*.

## Frankenstein is the scientist
**Victor Frankenstein** is the young scientist. **The creature has no name.** Calling the monster "Frankenstein" is a later mix-up.

## The creature learns
Abandoned by his maker, the creature hides in a hovel beside a cottage and secretly watches the **De Lacey** family. Through a chink in the wall he learns to speak and read as they teach their foreign guest. He finds three books: Milton's ***Paradise Lost***, ***Plutarch's Lives*** and Goethe's ***The Sorrows of Young Werther***. From them he learns about history, love, and God and creation.

When he finally faces Victor, he says:

> Remember that I am thy creature; I ought to be thy Adam, but I am rather the fallen angel, whom thou drivest from joy for no misdeed.

Like [Dantès and the Abbé Faria](../14-monte-cristo-and-napoleon/), he is educated in secret, in hiding, by a teacher on the other side of a wall. Both are rejected by the world and turn their learning toward revenge.

## The grunting monster
The flat-headed, bolt-necked, grunting monster most people picture comes from the **1931** film with **Boris Karloff**. In the novel, the creature is eloquent and quotes Milton.

## Key facts
- Tambora erupted April 1815, the largest eruption in recorded history
- 1816, the Year Without a Summer: June snow in New England, famine in Europe
- At the Villa Diodati, Byron's ghost-story challenge led to Frankenstein and The Vampyre
- Mary Shelley was 18; Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus was published in 1818
- Frankenstein is the scientist; the creature has no name
- The creature learns from the De Lacey family and Paradise Lost, Plutarch's Lives and Werther
- "I ought to be thy Adam, but I am rather the fallen angel"
- The grunting monster comes from the 1931 Boris Karloff film
