This is the sentence written for the GIS master deck on 9 November 2026:

> Picard measured France with a chain of triangles; a century later Mason and Dixon stretched chains and ten-foot rods straight along the ground instead, but both did the same thing underneath: turned star angles into miles on a round Earth.

Mason and Dixon did not use Picard's triangles. They measured length directly with chains and rods, and they got latitude from a zenith sector, a telescope aimed at stars overhead. What they share with [Picard](../73-picard-triangulation-1669/) is the idea of tying a measured ground distance to a measured angle in the sky. In 1768 their degree of latitude was set beside the French measures.

The [Mason-Dixon survey](../58-mason-dixon-survey/) lesson covers the zenith-sector latitudes, the chain lengths with slope taken out, and the 22.51-chain miss. This page is only the connection, and the warning not to say the geometry was Picard's.

The sentence and the reason for the wording are in [Mason and Dixon, and Picard (PDF)](/downloads/Mason_Dixon_Picard_Connection_Sentence_Faria_2026-11-09.pdf).

## Key facts
- Picard measured with a chain of triangles. Mason and Dixon measured length directly with chains and ten-foot rods.
- Both turned star angles into miles on a round Earth, by tying a ground length to a sky angle.
- Mason and Dixon got latitude with a zenith sector, not by repeating Picard's triangulation.
- In 1768 their degree of latitude was set beside the French measures.
