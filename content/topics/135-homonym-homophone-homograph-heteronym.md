*Homonym*, *homophone*, *homograph*, and *heteronym* are the four that collapse into each other in memory. They all start from Greek *onyma*, "name." The difference is in the front half, and in whether you are talking about sound, spelling, or both.

This is one of four lessons under [Confusing Word Terms](../133-confusing-word-terms/). The chart is the whole point.

## The chart

## Homonym, in two senses

**In the narrow sense, a homonym has the same spelling and the same sound, but a different meaning.**

*Bat* the animal and *bat* the club. *Rose* the flower and *rose* the past tense of *rise*. *Spring* the season, *spring* the coil, and *spring* the verb "to leap." Same letters, same sound, different jobs.

Greek *homos* means "same," plus *onyma*, "name": a same-name. Say it: HOM-uh-nim.

**In the loose sense, *homonym* is an umbrella for any words that share sound or spelling but not meaning.** That umbrella covers narrow homonyms, homophones, and homographs. Some schoolbooks go further and use *homonym* as if it simply meant *homophone*. Both looser habits are common. They are also why the words feel interchangeable.

When someone says "those are homonyms," it is fair to ask: same sound, same spelling, or both? On this page, the top-left cell is the narrow sense. The umbrella sense is named as such, and not used as if it were exact.

## Homophone

**A homophone has the same sound and a different meaning. The spelling is often different.**

*Night* and *knight*. *Flour* and *flower*. *To*, *too*, and *two*. *Write*, *right*, and *rite*. *Sea* and *see*. Greek *phōnē* is "sound," the same root as *telephone* and *phonetics*. A same-sound name.

Narrow homonyms (*bat* / *bat*) are homophones too, because they sound the same. In everyday talk, *homophone* usually points at the pairs that look different on the page.

Say it: HOM-uh-fone. Memory hook: *phone*, sound.

## Homograph

**A homograph has the same spelling and a different meaning. The sound may be the same or different.**

Greek *graphē* is "writing." A same-writing name. *Lead* (the metal) and *lead* (the verb) are homographs. So are *bat* and *bat*, which also sound alike. *Tear* in the eye and *tear* meaning "rip" are homographs with different sounds.

Say it: HOM-uh-graf. Memory hook: *graph*, writing.

If you remember only one split: *phone* is what you hear, *graph* is what you see.

## Heteronym

**A heteronym is a homograph with a different pronunciation as well as a different meaning.**

*Lead* pronounced "leed" (to guide) and *lead* pronounced "led" (the metal). *Wind* the moving air and *wind* meaning to coil. *Bass* the fish and *bass* the low voice. *Desert* the dry place and *desert* meaning to abandon. *Close* (near) and *close* (to shut). Greek *heteros* means "other": an other-name.

Every heteronym is a homograph. Not every homograph is a heteronym. *Bat* and *bat* share a sound, so they are not heteronyms.

Say it: HET-er-uh-nim. Memory hook: *hetero*, other.

## Capitonym

**A capitonym is a word that changes meaning, and sometimes pronunciation, when it is capitalized.**

*Polish* (from Poland, "POE-lish") and *polish* (to shine, "PAH-lish"). *August* the month ("AW-gust") and *august* meaning majestic ("aw-GUST"). *Job* the biblical figure, usually "johb," and *job* the work. *Reading* the town in England, "RED-ing," and *reading* a book. *Turkey* the country and *turkey* the bird share a sound and change only in sense.

The word is a later coinage from *capital* (as in capital letter) plus *-onym*. It is useful, and a little informal. *Polish* / *polish* is also a heteronym, because the sound changes too.

Say it: KAP-ih-tuh-nim.

## Key facts

- *Phone* is sound. *Graph* is writing. *Homo* is same. *Hetero* is other. *Onyma* is name.
- Narrow homonym: same spelling, same sound, different meaning (*bat* / *bat*). Loose *homonym*: an umbrella for that cell, for homophones, and for homographs.
- Homophone: same sound, often different spelling (*night* / *knight*). Homograph: same spelling, meaning differs, sound may or may not.
- Heteronym: same spelling, different sound, different meaning (*lead* / *lead*). It is a kind of homograph.
- A capitonym changes meaning, and sometimes sound, with a capital letter (*Polish* / *polish*).
