## The setting
After the Spanish-American War of **1898**, the **US Army occupied Cuba**. Yellow fever was killing more American soldiers than Spanish bullets had. Most doctors still believed in **miasma**, bad air, or that the disease spread through contact with the sick and their soiled clothes and bedding ("fomites").

## Carlos Finlay, the mosquito man
**Dr. Carlos Finlay**, a Cuban physician in Havana, proposed in **1881** that yellow fever was carried by a mosquito, the one now called ***Aedes aegypti***. He presented it to scientific meetings and ran experiments for years, but for **19 years** he was dismissed and mocked as "**the mosquito man**."

## The Yellow Fever Commission
In 1900 the Army sent the **Yellow Fever Commission**, led by Major **Walter Reed**, with **James Carroll**, **Jesse Lazear** and **Aristides Agramonte**. They met Finlay, who gave them his mosquito eggs.

- **Jesse Lazear** let infected mosquitoes bite himself and others. In **September 1900** he died of yellow fever after a bite, the **same month as the Galveston hurricane**.
- At **Camp Lazear**, named for him, volunteers (American soldiers and Spanish immigrants, who signed consent forms, among the first in medical history) were tested two ways. Some slept for weeks in a closed hut, in the **soiled bedding and clothing** of yellow fever victims; they **stayed healthy**. Others, in a clean, screened hut, were bitten by infected mosquitoes; they got sick.
- That **disproved the miasma and contact theories** and proved the mosquito.

## Gorgas clears Havana, then Panama
Army doctor **William Gorgas** wiped out mosquito breeding places in Havana: screens, draining standing water, oil on cisterns. By **1902** yellow fever was gone from the city. He then did the same in **Panama**, where yellow fever and malaria had helped defeat the French canal effort of the 1880s. That made the American **Panama Canal** possible (opened 1914).

## The irony of 1900
In the same year, the United States **ignored Cuba's hurricane warnings**, and Galveston drowned, while it **used Cuba's mosquito theory**, and **Walter Reed got the credit**. See [Galveston 1900](../09-galveston-1900/) and [Benito Viñes](../08-benito-vines/).

Latin America remembers Finlay: **December 3**, his birthday, is **Latin American Medicine Day** (Día de la Medicina Latinoamericana).

## After Reed
Walter Reed died in **1902** of a burst appendix (peritonitis). **Walter Reed General Hospital** opened in Washington in **1909**. In **2011** it merged with the naval hospital at **Bethesda**, Maryland, as the **Walter Reed National Military Medical Center**.

## Key facts
- Carlos Finlay proposed the mosquito (Aedes aegypti) in 1881; mocked for 19 years as "the mosquito man"
- Jesse Lazear died after an infected mosquito bit him, September 1900, the same month as Galveston
- At Camp Lazear, volunteers in soiled bedding stayed healthy: miasma disproved
- Gorgas cleared Havana of yellow fever by 1902, then Panama
- 1900 irony: the US ignored Cuba's hurricane warnings but used its mosquito theory, and Reed got the credit
- December 3, Finlay's birthday, is Latin American Medicine Day
- Reed died 1902 (burst appendix); Walter Reed hospital opened 1909, merged with Bethesda in 2011
