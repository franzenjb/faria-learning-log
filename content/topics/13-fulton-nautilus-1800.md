## The surprise
**Robert Fulton** (1765–1815), the American famous for the steamboat, built a **submarine first**. In 1800, living in France, he built the **Nautilus** and offered it to Napoleon's government as a weapon against the British navy, which was blockading France.

## The boat
- About 21 feet long, copper over an iron frame, shaped like a stretched egg.
- Hand-cranked propeller underwater; a folding mast and sail for the surface.
- Tested on the Seine and off the French coast at Le Havre and Brest; Fulton and a small crew stayed submerged for hours using compressed air.
- Its weapon was a "torpedo" (what we would now call a mine), towed and attached to an enemy hull.

## What happened
It worked, but it never sank a British ship: the British ships simply sailed away whenever it came near. Napoleon lost interest, and the French admirals thought it a dishonorable way to fight. Fulton then took the idea to the British, who also passed.

So he went home and turned to steam. His **Clermont** (officially *North River Steamboat*) began running on the Hudson from New York to Albany in **1807**, the first commercially successful steamboat. **The sub preceded the steamboat by seven years.**

## The name
*Nautilus* is Greek *nautilos*, "sailor" (see [Astronaut and nautical](../02-astronaut-and-nautical/)). Jules Verne named Captain Nemo's submarine after Fulton's boat, and has Nemo mention it. Later, the US Navy's first nuclear submarine, USS *Nautilus* (1954), carried the name on.

## Key facts
- Fulton built the Nautilus submarine for Napoleon in 1800, seven years before his steamboat
- Clermont steamboat on the Hudson, 1807
- Verne's Nautilus was named after Fulton's
