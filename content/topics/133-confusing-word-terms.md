These words all describe kinds of words, and most of them share one Greek ending: *-onym*, from *onyma*, "name." Once you can hear the root, the list stops being a blur. *Homonym* is a same-name. *Synonym* is a together-name. *Antonym* is an against-name. An *acronym* is a name made from the tips of other words.

This page is the map. The four lessons under it do the work.

## The memory hook

Greek *onyma* (also *onoma*) means "name." Add a front half, and you have a kind of name:

| Root | Meaning | It gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *onyma* | name | the *-nym* in all of these words |
| *homos* | same | homonym, homophone, homograph |
| *heteros* | other | heteronym |
| *phōnē* | sound | homophone, also telephone, phonetics |
| *graphē* | writing | homograph, also telegraph, geography |
| *syn* | together | synonym |
| *anti* | against | antonym |
| *akron* | tip, end | acronym |
| *eu* | good, well | euphemism |
| *neo* | new | neologism |

Say the front half out loud, then add "name." *HOM-uh-nim*. *SIN-uh-nim*. *AN-tuh-nim*. *EP-uh-nim*. The pronunciation sits on the same pattern.

A few of the terms on these pages are not *-nym* words at all: abbreviation, contraction, portmanteau, onomatopoeia, palindrome, anagram, malapropism, eggcorn, mondegreen, spoonerism, oxymoron, euphemism, neologism. They have their own hooks, and those are on the grouped pages.

## All the terms, in one list

**Shortened words**

- **Abbreviation**: any shortened form, such as *Dr.*, *lb.*, or *etc.*
- **Contraction**: letters dropped from the middle, usually with an apostrophe: *don't*, *I'll*, *o'clock*.
- **Acronym**: initials (or initial bits) pronounced as a word: *NASA*, *NATO*, *radar*, *scuba*.
- **Initialism**: initials pronounced letter by letter: *FBI*, *BBC*, *HTML*.
- **Backronym**: a phrase invented to fit letters that already exist, or a slogan built so the initials spell a word.

**Sound and spelling**

- **Homonym** (narrow): same spelling, same sound, different meaning: *bat* and *bat*.
- **Homonym** (loose): an umbrella for words that share sound or spelling but not meaning.
- **Homophone**: same sound, different meaning, often different spelling: *night* / *knight*.
- **Homograph**: same spelling, different meaning, possibly different sound: *lead* and *lead*.
- **Heteronym**: a homograph with a different sound: *wind* (the air) and *wind* (to coil).
- **Capitonym**: meaning, and sometimes sound, changes with a capital letter: *Polish* / *polish*.

**-nym names**

- **Synonym**: a together-name, a word with a near meaning: *big* / *large*.
- **Antonym**: an against-name, a word with the opposite meaning: *hot* / *cold*.
- **Contronym**, or **auto-antonym**: a word that is its own opposite: *sanction*, *cleave*, *oversight*.
- **Eponym**: a name taken from a person, or the person a thing is named after: *sandwich*, *boycott*.
- **Toponym**: a place-name, or a word taken from a place: *denim*, *dollar*, *spa*.
- **Demonym**: the word for the people of a place: *Parisian*, *Floridian*, *Liverpudlian*.
- **Retronym**: a new name for the old thing, after a new kind appears: *acoustic guitar*, *landline*.

**Wordplay and slips**

- **Portmanteau**: two words packed into one: *smog*, *brunch*, *motel*.
- **Onomatopoeia**: a word made to imitate a sound: *buzz*, *hiss*, *cuckoo*.
- **Palindrome**: a word or phrase that reads the same forwards and backwards: *kayak*; *A man, a plan, a canal: Panama*.
- **Anagram**: the same letters in a new order: *listen* / *silent*.
- **Malapropism**: the wrong word, close in sound, often comic: Mrs. Malaprop's "allegory on the banks of the Nile."
- **Eggcorn**: a slip that still makes a kind of sense: *egg corn* for *acorn*; *tow the line* for *toe the line*.
- **Mondegreen**: a misheard line, especially in a song or poem, that makes a new scene.
- **Spoonerism**: swapped first sounds: "the weight of rages" for "the rate of wages."
- **Oxymoron**: a compressed contradiction: *deafening silence*; Shakespeare's "cold fire."
- **Euphemism**: a milder word for a harsher fact: *passed away* for *died*.
- **Neologism**: a newly coined word: *robot*, *podcast*.

## Two cautions

First, *homonym* itself has two lives. In the strict sense it is only the top-left cell of the [sound-and-spelling chart](../135-homonym-homophone-homograph-heteronym/): same letters, same sound, different meaning. In loose schoolbook use it can mean any of the first three cells, and sometimes it is used as if it meant *homophone*. When someone says "homonym," it is fair to ask which sense they mean.

Second, a good story about letters is not the same as an etymology. *Posh* does not stand for "Port Out, Starboard Home." *SOS* does not stand for "Save Our Ship." *Golf* does not stand for "Gentlemen Only, Ladies Forbidden." Those are myths. The [shortened-words page](../134-shortened-words-abbreviation-to-backronym/) marks them as such.

## Key facts

- Most of these terms end in *-onym*, from Greek *onyma*, "name." Hear the front half, and the meaning usually follows.
- *Homos* is "same," *heteros* is "other," *phōnē* is "sound," *graphē* is "writing," *syn* is "together," *anti* is "against," *akron* is "tip."
- *Homonym* has a narrow sense (same spelling and same sound, different meaning) and a loose umbrella sense. The chart on the sound-and-spelling page keeps the cases apart.
- *Posh*, *SOS*, and *golf* are not acronyms. Those letter-stories are myths.
