## The man
**Thomas Stearns Eliot** (1888–1965)
- Born in **St. Louis**, Missouri, to an old New England family
- Studied at **Harvard**, then in Paris, Germany and Oxford
- Settled in **London in 1914**, just as World War I began
- Worked in the foreign department of **Lloyds Bank** (1917–1925), then as an editor at the publishers Faber
- Became a **British citizen** and joined the **Church of England** in **1927**
- **Nobel Prize in Literature, 1948**

## The Waste Land (1922)
Eliot's most famous long poem, written after the war and during a breakdown. It is a collage of voices, languages and quotations about a broken civilization. It opens:

> April is the cruellest month, breeding
> Lilacs out of the dead land, mixing
> Memory and desire, stirring
> Dull roots with spring rain.

This **reverses Chaucer**. *The Canterbury Tales* opens with April as the sweet month when showers bring the world back to life ("Whan that Aprill with his shoures soote") and people set off on pilgrimage. For Eliot, spring is cruel because it forces life and memory back into people who would rather stay numb.

Other famous lines:
- "**I will show you fear in a handful of dust.**"
- "**Those are pearls that were his eyes**," borrowed from Ariel's song in Shakespeare's *The Tempest* ("Full fathom five thy father lies... Those are pearls that were his eyes"), about a man drowned and changed by the sea.

## Cats
Eliot had a playful side. *Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats* (1939), light verse he wrote for his godchildren ("Old Possum" was his nickname from Ezra Pound), became Andrew Lloyd Webber's musical ***Cats*** (1981).

## Key facts
- T. S. Eliot, 1888 to 1965: born St. Louis, Harvard, London 1914, Lloyds Bank
- British citizen and Church of England, 1927; Nobel Prize 1948
- The Waste Land (1922): "April is the cruellest month" reverses Chaucer's sweet April
- "I will show you fear in a handful of dust"
- "Those are pearls that were his eyes" comes from The Tempest
- Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats (1939) became the musical Cats
