## Pronunciation
**Loci**: **LOH-sigh** (Latin for "places"; singular *locus*).

## Simonides and the collapsed banquet hall
The Greek poet **Simonides of Ceos** (around 556–468 BC) was performing at a banquet when he was called outside. While he was gone, the **roof of the hall collapsed**, crushing the guests so badly their families couldn't recognize them. Simonides found he could **identify every body by remembering where each guest had been sitting**.

He realized memory works best with **places**. To remember a list, picture a building you know, put a vivid image of each item in a spot along a route through it, then walk the route in your mind and pick them up in order.

## Cicero and the orators
The Roman orator **Cicero** tells the Simonides story in *De Oratore* (55 BC). Roman speakers memorized long speeches by placing each point in a room of an imagined house. Our phrase "**in the first place**" comes from this: the first point was literally in the first place along the walk.

**Topic** comes from Greek ***topos***, "**place**": a topic was the place where an argument was stored.

## Our practice memory palace
Walk through the house, from the front door:

1. **Front door**: **Eratosthenes** stands on the doormat with his stick, measuring its shadow. See [Eratosthenes](../18-eratosthenes-measures-the-earth/).
2. **Couch**: **Galileo** sprawled with his telescope, muttering "and yet it moves." See [Bruno and Galileo](../23-bruno-and-galileo/).
3. **Kitchen**: **Brunel** trying to squeeze the giant **Great Eastern** into the sink. See [the Great Eastern](../12-great-eastern-and-brunel/).
4. **Bedroom**: the **Nautilus** surfacing out of the bed, with **Verne** on deck. See [Twenty Thousand Leagues](../25-twenty-thousand-leagues/).
5. **Bathroom**: **Walter Reed** swatting mosquitoes over the tub. See [Reed and Finlay](../29-reed-finlay-yellow-fever/).

The sillier and more vivid the image, the better it sticks.

## Key facts
- Method of loci (LOH-sigh): memory by places
- Simonides of Ceos (around 500 BC) identified crushed banquet guests by where they sat
- Cicero tells the story; "in the first place" comes from it
- Topic comes from Greek topos, place
- Practice palace: door Eratosthenes, couch Galileo, kitchen Brunel, bedroom Nautilus, bathroom Reed
