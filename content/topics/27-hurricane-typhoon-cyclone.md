## Hurricane: a storm god
**Hurricane** comes from **Hurakán** (**hoo-rah-KAHN**), the storm god of the **Taíno**, the people Columbus met in the Caribbean. Spanish sailors took it home as *huracán* after Columbus's voyages, and English borrowed it from Spanish in the 1500s.

Farther west, the **Maya** had a creator god named **Huracan**, "one-legged," the heart of the sky. In the Maya creation book, the **Popol Vuh**, Huracan sends a great flood to wipe out an earlier race of failed people. Whether the Taíno and Maya names share a source is debated, but both tie the word to divine wind and flood.

## Typhoon: a great wind and a Greek monster
**Typhoon** probably comes from Chinese ***tai fung***, "great wind," as sailors heard it in the South China Sea. In English it blended with **Typhon**, the monstrous Greek storm giant, father of the winds, whom Zeus defeated and buried under Mount Etna. Two origins, one word.

## Cyclone: a circle
**Cyclone** comes from Greek ***kyklos***, "circle" or "wheel." It was coined in **1848** by **Henry Piddington**, a British sea captain in **Calcutta** who studied the storms of the Bay of Bengal and wanted one word for winds that whirl in a circle around a center.

Today they are all the same kind of storm: a **hurricane** in the Atlantic and northeast Pacific, a **typhoon** in the northwest Pacific, a **cyclone** in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific.

## The same circle in other words
- **Bicycle**: two (*bi-*) circles, two wheels
- **Cycle**, **recycle**: going around again
- **Encyclopedia**: from Greek ***enkyklios paideia***, "education in the circle," the well-rounded circle of subjects a free person should know: *en* (in) + *kyklos* (circle) + ***paideia*** (education, child-rearing)

## Paideia: the child root
*Paideia* comes from ***pais, paidos***, "child." The same root gives:
- **Pediatrician**: *paidos* + *iatros* (healer), a children's doctor
- **Pedagogy**: from ***paidagōgos***, literally "child-leader": in ancient Greece, the slave who walked a boy to school, carried his things and watched his manners. The word for the person who led the child became the word for the art of teaching.

## Key facts
- Hurricane: from Taíno storm god Hurakán (hoo-rah-KAHN), via Spanish huracán
- Maya creator god Huracan, "one-legged," sends the flood in the Popol Vuh
- Typhoon: probably Chinese tai fung, great wind, blended with the Greek monster Typhon
- Cyclone: Greek kyklos, circle; coined 1848 by Henry Piddington in Calcutta
- Encyclopedia: enkyklios paideia, education in the circle
- Pedagogy: from paidagogos, the slave who walked a boy to school
