## The book
**Nicolaus Copernicus** (1473–1543), a Polish astronomer and church canon, worked out a system with the **sun at the center** and the Earth as one planet circling it, spinning on its axis daily. He held back publishing for decades, partly fearing ridicule.

*De revolutionibus orbium coelestium* (*On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres*) was finally printed in **1543**. The story goes that the first printed copy was put in his hands on his deathbed, on May 24, 1543.

## Why it didn't cause an uproar at first
An unsigned preface, added by the Lutheran minister Andreas Osiander who oversaw the printing, said the system was just a mathematical tool for calculating, not a claim about reality. Copernicus was dead and couldn't object. The book was dense mathematics, read mostly by astronomers. The real fight came about 70 years later. See [Bruno and Galileo](../23-bruno-and-galileo/).

## Key facts
- Copernicus 1543: Earth moves around the sun, published as he was dying
- De revolutionibus orbium coelestium; Osiander's preface called it only a calculating tool
