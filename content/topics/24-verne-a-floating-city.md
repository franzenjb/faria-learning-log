## Where it came from
In spring 1867 Jules Verne and his brother Paul sailed from Liverpool to New York on Brunel's **Great Eastern**, then went up to see **Niagara Falls** before returning. Verne turned it into *Une ville flottante*, **A Floating City** (1871). The title is the ship itself: a city afloat, with thousands of people aboard.

## The storyline
The narrator (essentially Verne) boards the *Great Eastern*. He meets an old friend, **Captain Fabian MacElwin**, a British officer who is heartbroken: the woman he loved, **Ellen**, was forced to marry another man and has lost her reason from grief. It turns out that Ellen is aboard, with her cruel husband, **Harry Drake**, a gambler who married her for money.

Through the crossing, storms, shipboard life and gossip, tension builds between Fabian and Drake, until a duel is arranged in America. Before it can happen, Drake is killed by lightning during a storm at sea (a convenient act of heaven). At Niagara, Ellen's mind returns, and she and Fabian can be together.

## Is it any good today?
Honestly, the romance is melodrama and the plot is thin. What makes it worth reading is the **ship**: Verne's eyewitness description of the *Great Eastern*, her engines, her paddle wheels, her crowds, the storm that smashed her wheel, and life aboard the largest ship in the world. It's a short read and best taken as a travel journal by a man who loved machines. For the ship, see [the Great Eastern](../12-great-eastern-and-brunel/).

## Key facts
- A Floating City (1871) is based on Verne's own 1867 Great Eastern crossing
- The story: Fabian, Ellen and her villainous husband Harry Drake; ends at Niagara Falls
- Worth reading today mainly for the eyewitness picture of the Great Eastern
