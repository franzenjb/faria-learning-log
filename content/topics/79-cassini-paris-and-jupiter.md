This page was written from published biographical and observatory accounts. It is a reconstructed lesson, not a transcript, and it does not invent speech.

Giovanni Domenico Cassini was born in Perinaldo, then in the Republic of Genoa, in 1625. He died in Paris in 1712. In France he was Jean-Dominique Cassini. He is the first of four Cassinis who directed or extended the mapping of France. The spacecraft named Cassini is a later honor, not this man.

He made his name in Bologna, timing the moons of Jupiter and the rotation of Jupiter itself. Those moon tables are the clock Ole Rømer used when he argued that light takes time. That argument is the [Rømer lesson](/topics/65-romer-io-speed-of-light/). The idea that the moons could serve as a longitude clock, and why a long telescope failed on a rolling deck, is the [Galileo lesson](/topics/46-galileo/). On land the same clock worked. Cassini's ephemerides, predictions of when a moon would leave Jupiter's shadow as seen from Paris, let an observer elsewhere compare the local time of the same event and read a longitude.

At the new Observatoire de Paris he turned to Saturn. He found four moons, Iapetus, Rhea, Tethys, and Dione, and the dark gap in the rings still called the Cassini Division. Those discoveries are sky measurements. They are not the triangle plate of Picard.

The mapping of France used both kinds of work. Picard and Philippe de La Hire observed eclipses of Jupiter's moons along the west coast, against Cassini's Paris predictions, and the coast moved east on the map published in 1693. Separately, in 1683, Cassini started south from Paris and La Hire started north, to carry a meridian through the kingdom. Picard's own degree, from Malvoisine to Amiens, is the [meridian lesson](/topics/73-picard-triangulation-1669/). The later chain is a network of many triangles, told on the [geodesy page](/topics/82-geodetic-networks/). Do not fold the 1671 plate, the 1693 coast, and the Cassini meridian into one drawing.

His son Jacques, his grandson César-François, and his great-grandson Jean-Dominique continued pieces of the French survey. A family of directors is not one person who lived two centuries.

## Key facts
- Cassini was born in Perinaldo in 1625 and died in Paris in 1712. Four Cassinis worked on the French survey. They are not one man.
- His Jupiter-moon tables gave Paris a predicted time. A matching eclipse elsewhere gave a longitude.
- The Cassini Division is a gap in Saturn's rings. The 1693 coast map is a different product, drawn from those eclipse longitudes.
- The 1683 meridian extension is not Picard's 1669-1670 plate.
