## Who he was
**Father Benito Viñes** (BEN-ee-toh VEEN-yes), a Spanish Jesuit priest, became director of the observatory at **Belén College** (Colegio de Belén) in Havana, Cuba, in 1870. Cubans called him **Padre Huracán**, Father Hurricane.

## How he and his team became experts
Nobody trained them; there was no such field yet. They became experts the way science always works: patient, careful observation, many times a day, for years.

- **Constant measurements.** Barometer, wind direction and speed, temperature and humidity, recorded hour after hour.
- **The clouds.** Viñes studied the high, thin **cirrus** clouds that fan out from a hurricane's top. He worked out that they spread out ahead of the storm, often a day or two before it arrives, and their streaks point back toward its center.
- **Wind shifts and the barometer.** The way the wind swings and the pressure drops told him which side of the storm a place was on and whether it was approaching.
- **A network.** Viñes set up a network of observers across Cuba and the Caribbean who sent their readings to Havana by telegraph. Combined, they showed the storm's track.
- **Writing it down.** He published his rules for reading storms (*Apuntes relativos a los huracanes de las Antillas*, 1877), which ship captains used across the Caribbean.

His first public, scientific hurricane warning went out in **September 1875**, and it saved lives.

## Could he "see" the spin?
No, and this is an important correction. Nobody could see a hurricane's spiral from the ground; that wasn't possible until satellites in the 1960s. What Viñes did was read **indirect signs**: which way the cirrus clouds streamed, how the barometer fell, how the wind shifted. From those he reasoned out where the center was and where it was heading. It was inference, not sight, which is what makes it so impressive.

## After Viñes
Viñes died in 1893. The Belén Observatory carried on his methods, and in September 1900 Cuban forecasters warned of the storm that destroyed Galveston. See [Galveston 1900](../09-galveston-1900/).

## Key facts
- Father Benito Viñes (Belén College, Havana): cirrus clouds fan out ahead of a hurricane and point to its center
- Viñes issued the first scientific hurricane warning in 1875, using an observer network linked by telegraph
- Nicknamed Padre Huracán; nobody could see the spiral until satellites
