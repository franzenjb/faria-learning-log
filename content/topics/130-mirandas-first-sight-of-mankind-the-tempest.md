> O wonder!
> How many goodly creatures are there here!
> How beauteous mankind is! O brave new world,
> That has such people in't!

— Miranda, *The Tempest*, Act 5, Scene 1

Miranda, raised on the island since she was three with only her father Prospero and Caliban for company, says this on first seeing the shipwrecked lords. Prospero answers drily, "'Tis new to thee."

Aldous Huxley borrowed the phrase for the title of *Brave New World* in 1932. Miranda means it as wonder. Huxley used it as a warning.

**Question.** When have you called something new only because it was new to you?

## The Last Word

The world is only new if you have never seen it.

## Key facts

- The speech is Miranda's, from *The Tempest*, Act 5, Scene 1. The wording here follows a standard modernized text of the 1623 First Folio.
- Miranda was three when she and Prospero landed. She has grown up on the island with her father and Caliban as the only people she has really known.
- Prospero's reply is "'Tis new to thee."
- Aldous Huxley took "brave new world" as the title of his novel in 1932.
