> O wonder!
> How many goodly creatures are there here!
> How beauteous mankind is! O brave new world,
> That has such people in't!

— Miranda, *The Tempest*, Act 5, Scene 1

Miranda, raised on the island since she was three with only her father Prospero and Caliban for company, says this on first seeing the shipwrecked lords. Prospero answers drily, "'Tis new to thee."

Aldous Huxley borrowed the phrase for the title of *Brave New World* in 1932. Miranda means it as wonder. Huxley used it as a warning.

## Prospero's Farewell

A little earlier, in Act 4, Scene 1, the wedding masque of spirits dissolves, and Prospero turns to Ferdinand with this:

> Our revels now are ended. These our actors,
> As I foretold you, were all spirits and
> Are melted into air, into thin air;
> And like the baseless fabric of this vision,
> The cloud-capped towers, the gorgeous palaces,
> The solemn temples, the great globe itself,
> Yea, all which it inherit, shall dissolve,
> And, like this insubstantial pageant faded,
> Leave not a rack behind. We are such stuff
> As dreams are made on, and our little life
> Is rounded with a sleep.

Then, when the play is over, Prospero steps forward and asks us to finish what his magic no longer can. This is the complete Epilogue, twenty lines:

> Now my charms are all o'erthrown,
> And what strength I have's mine own,
> Which is most faint. Now 'tis true
> I must be here confined by you,
> Or sent to Naples. Let me not,
> Since I have my dukedom got
> And pardoned the deceiver, dwell
> In this bare island by your spell,
> But release me from my bands
> With the help of your good hands.
> Gentle breath of yours my sails
> Must fill, or else my project fails,
> Which was to please. Now I want
> Spirits to enforce, art to enchant,
> And my ending is despair,
> Unless I be relieved by prayer,
> Which pierces so that it assaults
> Mercy itself, and frees all faults.
> As you from crimes would pardoned be,
> Let your indulgence set me free.

The speech is twenty lines in rhyming couplets of short lines. It is among Shakespeare's most famous epilogues. It is not the longest. The epilogue to *2 Henry IV*, spoken in prose, is longer.

The images do the work of the plea. The "good hands" are the audience's applause, which breaks the spell and lets him leave the bare island of the stage. The "gentle breath" is their praise or cheers, filling his sails. "Indulgence" is their pardon — and a pun on a church indulgence, a formal forgiveness.

For the story around these speeches — Sycorax, Caliban, Ariel's tasks, and why Prospero breaks his staff — see [The Tempest: Prospero, Ariel, and the Price of Power](../131-the-tempest-prospero-ariel-and-the-price-of-power/).

**Question.** When have you called something new only because it was new to you?

**Question.** If applause can break a spell, what, in a life you know, would count as the good hands that set someone free?

## Key facts

- Miranda's speech is from *The Tempest*, Act 5, Scene 1. The wording here follows the Folger Shakespeare Library's modern-spelling text, based on the 1623 First Folio.
- Miranda was three when she and Prospero landed. She has grown up on the island with her father and Caliban as the only people she has really known. Prospero's reply is "'Tis new to thee."
- Aldous Huxley took "brave new world" as the title of his novel in 1932.
- "Our revels now are ended... We are such stuff as dreams are made on, and our little life is rounded with a sleep" is Prospero's, Act 4, Scene 1, after the masque of spirits fades.
- The Epilogue, also spoken by Prospero, is 20 lines of rhyming couplets. It is among the most famous of Shakespeare's epilogues, not the longest; the prose epilogue to *2 Henry IV* is longer.
- "Good hands" are the audience's applause; "gentle breath" is their praise or cheers filling the sails; "indulgence" is their pardon, with a pun on a church indulgence.
