**QED** stands for the Latin *quod erat demonstrandum*, "which was to be shown." You write it at the end of a proof to mark that you have reached exactly the statement you set out to prove.

## From Euclid to Latin
Euclid ended his proofs with the Greek phrase *ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι* (*hoper edei deixai*), the same idea. Latin translators of Euclid turned it into *quod erat demonstrandum*, and later writers shortened that to QED.

## The square
Many modern books use a small square, ∎, instead of the letters. It is called the tombstone, or the Halmos box, after the mathematician Paul Halmos.

## How to say it
Most people just say the letters: "Q-E-D," *kyoo-ee-dee*.

The Latin, read in an English way, is *kwod AIR-at dem-un-STRAN-dum*. Classical Latin is closer to *kwod EH-rot day-mohn-STRAHN-doom*.

## Key facts
- QED is Latin *quod erat demonstrandum*, "which was to be shown."
- It marks the end of a proof: you have shown exactly what you set out to prove.
- Euclid closed proofs with the Greek *ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι* (*hoper edei deixai*).
- Many books now use a square, ∎, called the tombstone or Halmos box, after Paul Halmos.
- Said letter by letter: *kyoo-ee-dee*.
