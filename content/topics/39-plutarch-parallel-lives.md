## Who he was
Plutarch (about **46 to 120 AD**) was a Greek from the small town of **Chaeronea**, living under Roman rule. He was also a **Roman citizen**. For about the last **thirty years of his life** he served at **Delphi** as one of the **two priests of Apollo**.

## The Parallel Lives
He wrote the *Lives* in **pairs**: a famous Greek matched with a famous Roman.
- Theseus and Romulus
- Alexander and Caesar
- Demosthenes and Cicero

Most pairs end with a short **"comparison."** **Twenty-three pairs survive, plus four single lives.**

## Not histories but lives
The key to reading him is his own statement at the start of *Alexander*:

> **"It is not histories I am writing, but lives."**

He says a small thing, "a phrase or a jest," often reveals a man's character better than battles in which thousands die. He isn't a historian. He's a **moralist who uses great men as mirrors**, so readers can look at them and fix their own character.

## Why the creature reads him
That's why Mary Shelley gave the book to the creature in *Frankenstein*. From Plutarch he learns about public virtue. He says the book taught him "high thoughts":

> "I felt the greatest ardour for virtue rise within me."

He admires the **lawgivers Numa, Solon and Lycurgus** more than the conquerors. *Paradise Lost* gives him the cosmic story, of being made and cast out. Plutarch gives him the social one, a picture of how a good man lives among other people. He learns both and still has nobody to live among.

## Plutarch and Shakespeare
**Thomas North's 1579 English translation** was Shakespeare's source for ***Julius Caesar***, ***Antony and Cleopatra*** and ***Coriolanus***. Shakespeare's Cleopatra on her barge on the Cydnus follows North's prose almost line for line, set into verse.

## Question to think about
Plutarch thinks a small, unguarded moment reveals character better than a great deed. In disaster work, do you learn more about a person from how they run the big operation, or from some small thing they do at 2 a.m. in a shelter?

## Key facts
- Plutarch: c. 46–120 AD, from Chaeronea; Greek, and a Roman citizen; priest of Apollo at Delphi for about his last 30 years.
- *Parallel Lives*: 23 surviving pairs plus 4 single lives, most pairs with a closing comparison.
- "It is not histories I am writing, but lives." (*Alexander*, opening)
- The creature in *Frankenstein*: "I felt the greatest ardour for virtue rise within me."
- North's 1579 translation was the source for *Julius Caesar*, *Antony and Cleopatra* and *Coriolanus*.
