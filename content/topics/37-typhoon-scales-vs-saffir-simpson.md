## The question
Looking at Wikipedia's RSMC Tokyo typhoon scale, Jeff asked: does Saffir–Simpson not apply to typhoons? The answer: it does, but not officially.

## RSMC Tokyo: the official scale
The **Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA)**, acting as **RSMC Tokyo**, is the World Meteorological Organization's official warning center for the western North Pacific. Its scale uses **10-minute sustained winds**:

- **Tropical Depression**: ≤33 kt (≤61 km/h)
- **Tropical Storm**: 34–47 kt (62–88 km/h)
- **Severe Tropical Storm**: 48–63 kt (89–117 km/h)
- **Typhoon**: 64–84 kt (118–156 km/h)
- **Very Strong Typhoon**: 85–104 kt (157–193 km/h)
- **Violent Typhoon**: ≥105 kt (≥194 km/h)

## The American view: JTWC
The US **Joint Typhoon Warning Center (JTWC)** measures **1-minute sustained winds**, which read about **12% higher** than 10-minute averages for the same storm. JTWC unofficially applies the **Saffir–Simpson** categories to typhoons, and calls any storm of **≥130 kt** (1-minute) a **super typhoon**, roughly a strong Category 4 to a Category 5.

## One storm, two labels
**Typhoon Haiyan** (called **Yolanda** in the Philippines), 2013, was a **Violent Typhoon** to JMA and a **Category 5 super typhoon** to JTWC. Same storm, different averaging window, different vocabulary.

## Every basin has its own scale
- **India (IMD)** ranks North Indian Ocean storms in steps up to **super cyclonic storm**.
- **Australia (Bureau of Meteorology)** has its own **Category 1–5** scale based on **wind gusts**, not sustained winds, so an Australian Category 3 is not the same as a US Category 3.

## Comparison table: Tokyo vs Saffir–Simpson
Rule of thumb for converting: **1-minute ≈ 10-minute × 1.14**. The matches below are approximate.

| Tokyo (JMA) class | 10-min winds | ≈ 1-min equivalent | Saffir–Simpson (1-min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical Depression | ≤33 kt | ≤38 kt | Tropical Depression (≤33 kt) |
| Tropical Storm | 34–47 kt | 39–54 kt | Tropical Storm (34–63 kt) |
| Severe Tropical Storm | 48–63 kt | 55–72 kt | Strong Tropical Storm to low Cat 1 |
| Typhoon | 64–84 kt | 73–96 kt | Cat 1 to Cat 2 (64–95 kt) |
| Very Strong Typhoon | 85–104 kt | 97–119 kt | Cat 3 to low Cat 4 |
| Violent Typhoon | ≥105 kt | ≥120 kt | Strong Cat 4 to Cat 5 (≥137 kt) |

Saffir–Simpson reference ranges (1-minute winds):

| Category | Winds |
|---|---|
| Cat 1 | 64–82 kt |
| Cat 2 | 83–95 kt |
| Cat 3 | 96–112 kt |
| Cat 4 | 113–136 kt |
| Cat 5 | ≥137 kt |

The US **super typhoon** threshold (≥130 kt 1-minute) falls inside Tokyo's **Violent Typhoon** class.

## Why 1-minute vs 10-minute matters
Wind is never steady: it comes in **gusts and lulls**. Averaging over a longer window dilutes the gusts with the lulls, so a 10-minute average always gives a **lower number** than a 1-minute average. The 1-minute figure reads about **12% higher**. The US uses 1-minute winds; the **WMO standard is 10-minute**.

- **Same storm, different numbers:** 100 kt (10-min) ≈ 114 kt (1-min). That is **Cat 3 vs Cat 4** for the same wind.
- **Damage:** structures fail in the strongest bursts, and wind force scales with **speed squared**, so ~12% more speed ≈ **~25% more force** (1.12² ≈ 1.25).
- **Records:** always check the averaging period before comparing storms from different agencies or basins.

## Key facts
- Saffir–Simpson applies to typhoons only unofficially; RSMC Tokyo (JMA) is the WMO's official western North Pacific center.
- JMA uses 10-minute sustained winds; Saffir–Simpson and JTWC use 1-minute winds, about 12% higher.
- JMA scale: TD ≤33 kt, TS 34–47, STS 48–63, Typhoon 64–84, Very Strong 85–104, Violent ≥105 kt.
- JTWC "super typhoon" = ≥130 kt 1-minute winds, roughly strong Cat 4 to Cat 5.
- Haiyan (Yolanda) 2013: Violent Typhoon to JMA, Category 5 super typhoon to JTWC.
- India's IMD tops out at "super cyclonic storm."
- Australia's BoM Categories 1–5 are based on gusts and don't match US categories.

- Saffir–Simpson (1-min): Cat 1 64–82 kt, Cat 2 83–95, Cat 3 96–112, Cat 4 113–136, Cat 5 ≥137.
- Tokyo's Typhoon ≈ Cat 1–2; Very Strong Typhoon ≈ Cat 3 to low Cat 4; Violent Typhoon ≈ strong Cat 4 to Cat 5.
