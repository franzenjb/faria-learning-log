Kepler did not guess Mars's orbit from one night. Mars returns to the same place in its path about every 687 days. Earth has moved. If you know where Earth is, two lines of sight to that same Martian place cross, and you have a point in space. Angles measured against the stars give the directions. One angle plus a guessed radius is not enough. Kepler also had to repair Earth's own orbit before the crossings would sit still. Many pairs, not one clever triangle, drew the curve. It is an ellipse, with the Sun at a focus.

Opposition and conjunction are easy to mix up with a measurement. At opposition the order is Sun, Earth, Mars, with Earth in the middle. Mars is up all night and near its brightest. At conjunction the order is Earth, Sun, Mars. Mars is lost in the glare. Nothing is eclipsed. A straight alignment is not, by itself, a distance. Hipparchus's lesson uses a short baseline on the Earth. Here the baseline is Earth's whole orbit, the same stick that, once it has a length in miles, carries the [transit lesson](/topics/52-venus-transit-to-cepheid-distances/) outward.

An early illustrated sheet of this ellipse, made to be obvious in a small picture, stretched the truth and said so in a caption. It was not a printer's accident. On that sheet Mars's semiaxes were 247 and 199 drawing units, and Earth's orbital radius was 66. The ratio is about 3.74, against the real ratio of semimajor axes, about 1.524. The drawing is about 2.46 times too stretched. Its eccentricity is about 0.59, against Mars's real 0.093, roughly six times too oval. The Sun sits about 2.2 of those Earth-radii from the center. In the sky the offset is about 0.14 AU. If you treat the drawn Earth radius as 1 AU, the sheet's nearest and farthest distances are about 1.53 and 5.96 AU. The real ones are about 1.38 and 1.67 AU.

Earth's orbital radius ought to be about 66 percent of Mars's semimajor axis, not about 27 percent. That is why Earth looks tiny on the old panel. The tininess is the exaggeration. It is the wrong reason for the eye to think Earth is small. The planet dots on that sheet were 14 and 12 units across, a ratio near 1.17. Mars's diameter is about 6,792 kilometers and Earth's about 12,756, a ratio near 0.53. Dots are names, not worlds.

It is the same method, not a second theory. Mars returns to the same place in its orbit about every 687 days, which is one Martian year, not the gap between oppositions. Earth has moved. Distant stars give a shared direction. Draw each sightline from the Earth's place on that date. Where the sightlines cross is one position of Mars. The Earth-to-Earth chord sets the scale. On the teaching circle the Sun-to-Mars distance comes out about 1.5 times the mean Earth-Sun distance, and that distance changes around the orbit. A star direction by itself is not a distance. Three points in a straight line are not this triangle. Many such pairs, at other places in the orbit, are what let the path show itself as an ellipse with the Sun at a focus. The sheet exaggerates the flattening on purpose and says so. Opposition puts Earth between the Sun and Mars. Conjunction puts the Sun between Earth and Mars, and the glare hides Mars. Neither alignment is a distance. Mars is not hidden for half an orbit forever. Visibility changes with where Earth stands.

## The family, and whose life was a soldier's

This section is a reconstructed account from the Dictionary of Scientific Biography and later reports of the trial. It is not a transcript, and it does not quote Kepler's private character sketches as if they were courtroom testimony.

Johannes Kepler was born at Weil der Stadt on 27 December 1571 and died at Regensburg on 15 November 1630. His father was Heinrich Kepler. When Johannes was three, Heinrich joined mercenary soldiers to fight in the Dutch wars. That choice disgraced the household. He came back around 1576, served again with the armies in the Low Countries, and in 1588 left the family for good. The mercenary was the father. It was not a family trade, and it was not a label for the mother, the grandparents, or the son.

His mother was Katharina Guldenmann. The Dictionary of Scientific Biography says Kepler spent many months between 1617 and 1620 preparing a legal defense when she was accused and tried as a witch. She was imprisoned in August 1620 and, after he had taken on the defense, acquitted and released in 1621. The trial is hers. It is not a story in which the whole house was accused.

The Mars triangles do not depend on that trial. They are stated here so the soldier and the accusation stay attached to the people they belong to.

## Key facts
- A repeated heliocentric place of Mars, seen from two known positions of Earth, fixes a point. Many points make the ellipse. One angle is not enough.
- Opposition puts Earth between the Sun and Mars. Conjunction hides Mars in the Sun's glare. Neither alignment is a distance by itself.
- The exaggerated sheet has orbital ratio about 3.74 instead of 1.52, and eccentricity about 0.59 instead of 0.093. Earth's orbit should be about 66 percent of Mars's semimajor axis, not 27 percent. The dots are not sizes: Mars is about half Earth's diameter.
- Heinrich Kepler was the mercenary. He left for good in 1588. Katharina Kepler was tried as a witch, imprisoned in 1620, and released after acquittal in 1621.
