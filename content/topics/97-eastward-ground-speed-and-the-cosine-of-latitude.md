Stand still and you are still moving. Earth turns once a day, and it carries you east with it. How fast depends on where you stand. The rule is one cosine.

## 1. The equator: about 1,040 miles per hour

The equator is about 24,900 miles around. Earth turns once against the stars in one [sidereal day](../50-sidereal-day/), about 23 hours 56 minutes.

24,900 miles ÷ 23.93 hours ≈ **1,040 miles per hour**.

That is the eastward ground speed of a point on the equator.

## 2. Farther north, a smaller daily circle

At latitude φ (the Greek letter phi), you do not ride around the equator. You ride around a smaller circle, parallel to it.

The radius of that circle is your distance from Earth's spin axis. If Earth's radius is *R*, that distance is **R cos φ**. One trip around a smaller circle, in the same day, is a slower ride.

So the speed shrinks by the same factor:

> Eastward ground speed = (speed at the equator) × (cosine of latitude)

In symbols: **v = v(equator) × cos φ = 1,040 × cos φ** miles per hour.

## 3. The unit circle shows why

Draw a circle of radius 1. The center is Earth's center, *O*. The vertical line is Earth's spin axis. Put yourself at *P*, at angle φ up from the equator.

Drop straight down from *P* to the equator line. Call that point *Q*. The length *OQ* is cos φ. It is also how far *P* sits from the spin axis.

- At **20°**, cos 20° ≈ 0.94. *Q* is near the edge. You keep 94% of the equator's speed.
- At **60°**, cos 60° = 0.5. *Q* is halfway out. You keep exactly half.

## 4. The table

| Latitude | cos φ | Eastward ground speed |
|---|---|---|
| 0° (equator) | 1 | 1,040 mph |
| 20° | 0.94 | 977 mph |
| 40° | 0.77 | 797 mph |
| 60° | 0.50 | 520 mph |
| 80° | 0.17 | 180 mph |
| 90° (North Pole) | 0 | 0 mph |

Every number is 1,040 × cos φ. At the pole your daily circle has shrunk to a point. You turn in place.

The sine and cosine come from the [ladder lesson](../77-ladder-sine-cosine-and-tangent/): cosine is the base of the triangle, sine is the height.

## Key facts
- Earth's spin carries the equator east at about 1,040 mph: 24,900 miles in one sidereal day.
- At latitude φ you are R cos φ from Earth's axis, so your daily circle shrinks by cos φ.
- Eastward ground speed = (speed at the equator) × (cosine of latitude): v = 1,040 × cos φ.
- 0° 1,040 mph; 20° 977; 40° 797; 60° 520; 80° 180; North Pole 0.
- On the unit circle, the base OQ is cos φ: 0.94 at 20°, 0.5 at 60°.
