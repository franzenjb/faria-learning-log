The Cepheid Edwin Hubble identified in the Andromeda galaxy, M31, in 1923 is called M31-V1, Hubble's Variable Number One. It pulses with a period of about 31.4 days. Hubble's own figure was 31.415 days.

He spotted it on a Mount Wilson plate taken on the night of October 5 to 6, 1923. He first marked it "N" for nova, then crossed that out and wrote "VAR!" for variable.

With Henrietta Leavitt's period-luminosity relation, that period became a distance. Andromeda was far outside the Milky Way. The road that makes a Cepheid a yardstick, from a Venus transit to Leavitt's straight line, is [From a Venus transit to the Cepheids](../52-venus-transit-to-cepheid-distances/). Leavitt's story is on [The Harvard Computers](../51-harvard-computers/).
