Jeff's 7 November sitting ran from Cook's charts to a thumb held up at the Moon, with the people and the ships in between. The transit of 1769 is here as a campaign, beside the longer ladder already told on [6 November](/sessions/2026-11-06/). Each lesson has its own page and its own PDF. The Moon lesson also carries a supplementary map, not a lesson of its own: [the 2,100-mile circle on the United States](/downloads/Moon_Diameter_Four_Cities_US_Map_Faria_2026-11-07.pdf).

One question from the sitting: who observed the 1769 transit better, Dixon in Norway or Chappe in Baja California? [Dixon's lesson](/topics/57-jeremiah-dixon/) sets out the weather, the instruments, and the later praise. [The Venus lesson](/topics/55-venus-transits-1761-1769/) has the Baja dates and what the 18 June eclipse did not settle. Neither page names a winner. A further page turns a difference of local solar time into longitude, so an entry like Chappe's hours west of Paris can be read as an angle.
