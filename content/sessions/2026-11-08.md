The new pages this day are the wind-force curve, the feather and the anvil, Picard's meridian, and Tycho Brahe on Hven. Each of those keeps this date as the day it was written.

Other lessons were opened again. Their original days stay where they were. Cook's page now records that the watch on the second voyage was Kendall's K1, a copy, not Harrison's own H4. The chronometer section still lives on the longitude lesson. Pages that do not exist yet, including a life of Kepler's family, Cassini, Maurolico, Snell, a metric system, and a geodetic network beyond Picard's own arc, are not listed, because a day cannot point at a lesson that has not been written.

The falling sheet is [A feather and an anvil falling through air (PDF)](/downloads/Falling_Objects_Velocity_Time_Curves_Faria_2026-11-08.pdf). The 458-page binder was assembled before the Picard lesson and the Tycho lesson. It does not contain those pages, and it has not been rebuilt.
