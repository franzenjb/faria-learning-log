title: "Nov 8: wind force squared, and a feather beside an anvil"

Jeff's 8 November question started from a hurricane wind field and from the habit of calling the force exponential. On a comparable wall, in comparable air, the push follows the square of the speed. Equal steps in miles per hour add larger pieces of force, and they do not multiply that force by one fixed factor. The fixed factor is the exponential pattern, the one in compound interest. The power flowing in the wind is a third measurement, and it follows the cube. The drawing of the square is [the velocity-squared curve (PDF)](/downloads/Wind_Force_Velocity_Squared_Curve_Faria_2026-11-08.pdf). Both axes are linear and begin at zero.

A second sheet the same day sets a feather beside an anvil. Both start from rest with the same acceleration, 9.81 metres per second squared. Air resistance then separates them. The speeds 2 and 60 metres per second are chosen for the drawing. They are not measurements. Terminal speed means the acceleration has ended, and the object keeps falling. The chart is [A feather and an anvil falling through air (PDF)](/downloads/Falling_Objects_Velocity_Time_Curves_Faria_2026-11-08.pdf).
