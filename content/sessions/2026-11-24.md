title: "Nov 24: Ancient Parallels, Socrates, Why?, Aristotle, the Doctrine of the Mean, Dionysius Exiguus, Classical Athens, Miranda, The Tempest, and the Antikythera Mechanism"
---
Ten lessons from the same day. First, [Ancient Parallels: How Mesopotamian Cultures Shaped the Old Testament](/topics/123-ancient-parallels-mesopotamia-old-testament/): four story parallels (the flood, creation, law, and wisdom), a Babylonian field problem, and a coda on Omar Khayyam and FitzGerald's *Rubáiyát*. Then [Socrates: The Gadfly of Athens](/topics/124-socrates-gadfly-of-athens/): his life and method, the trial of 399 BCE, the refusal to escape, the last words, and a short look at the cycle of governments in Plato's *Republic*. Then [Why?: Questioning Authority from Socrates to Darwin](/topics/125-why-questioning-authority-socrates-to-darwin/): one question followed from Socrates through Aquinas, Erasmus, Luther, and Galileo to Darwin. Then [Aristotle: Master of Those Who Know](/topics/126-aristotle-master-of-those-who-know/): his life and method, the ethics of the mean, the four causes, the break from Plato, and a section on where he was wrong and why those errors lasted. Then [Aristotle's Doctrine of the Mean: Virtue Between Two Extremes](/topics/127-aristotle-doctrine-of-the-mean/): seven virtues, each between too little and too much, treated as everyday tools. Then [How a Monk Set Year One: Dionysius Exiguus](/topics/128-dionysius-exiguus-how-a-monk-set-year-one/): a sixth-century monk labeling Easter tables, a year one a few years late, what Bede did and did not see, and how the number spread into law. Then [Classical Athens: Genius Within Walking Distance](/topics/129-classical-athens-genius-within-walking-distance/): a city you could walk, the Parthenon, the theater, the Agora, Plato's Academy and Aristotle's Lyceum, and a timeline showing that it was not one golden afternoon. Then [Miranda's First Sight of Mankind — The Tempest](/topics/130-mirandas-first-sight-of-mankind-the-tempest/): the speech she gives on first seeing the shipwrecked lords, Prospero's dry reply, and the later life of the phrase "brave new world," Prospero's "revels" speech, and the complete Epilogue. Then [The Tempest: Prospero, Ariel, and the Price of Power](/topics/131-the-tempest-prospero-ariel-and-the-price-of-power/): Sycorax and the cloven pine, Caliban's claim, each of Ariel's tasks, and why Prospero breaks his staff. Then [The Antikythera Mechanism: The World's First Computer](/topics/132-antikythera-mechanism-worlds-first-computer/): a hand-cranked bronze machine from a wreck dated to about 70–60 BC, the 1900 find and the 2005 X-ray scans, the pin-and-slot that copies the Moon's uneven pace, and a 2021 reconstruction of the front cosmos display.
