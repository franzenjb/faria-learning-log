Following up on Delphi: Jeff noted that in a thousand years some might consider Christianity a myth, and asked whether every major religion eventually becomes one. The full discussion is in the "Ancient Greek Religion" topic, starting at the section on religion and myth.

## Does every major religion become a myth?
Not every one, but most do. The surprise is that the oldest living religions are also the ones that never did.

**The ones that became myth** usually had their gods tied to a state or a people that later fell: Egypt's gods went with the pharaohs, Mesopotamia's with Babylon, Greece's and Rome's with the empire, the Norse gods with Viking kingship, the Aztec gods with the Spanish conquest. When the temples, priests and cities that held a religion up are gone, its stories survive only as literature, and that's when people start calling them "myth."

**The ones that didn't:**
- **Hinduism** has roots in the Vedas from about 1500 BC, older than Delphi. About a billion people still practice it.
- **Judaism** is about 3,000 years old. It survived losing its Temple, twice, and its homeland, because it moved its center from a building to a book and a way of life.
- **Zoroastrianism**, the religion of Persia, has only about 100,000 to 200,000 followers. It is dwindling but still alive after about 3,000 years.

**The pattern:** religions held together by a text, a community and a daily practice outlast religions held together by temples and kings. Greek religion had no scripture and no church to carry it once Delphi went quiet. Judaism had both.

Christianity, Islam and Buddhism have exactly those survival traits (scripture, communities everywhere, portable practice), but nobody can say what a thousand years will bring.
