## Polaris
- A **triple** (Aa F7Ib Cepheid, Ab close dwarf imaged by Hubble in 2006, B found by Herschel in 1779), not a single star.
- **Altitude of Polaris = latitude**; it sits ~0.66° from the true pole.
- **Precession** (~26,000 y): Thuban in the pyramid age, Pytheas saw an empty pole, Polaris closest ~2100, then Gamma Cephei, then Vega ~14,500.
- **Cepheids** (SEF-ee-id): Leavitt's 1912 period–luminosity law; Polaris period ~3.97 days, amplitude and period both misbehaving.
- Distance: Hipparcos ~432 ly vs Turner ~323 ly vs Gaia ~446.5 ly; the nearest Cepheid calibrates the cosmic distance ladder.
- Names: cynosure, lodestar, Stella Maris, Dhruva, Arabic axle/needle, Old English Tir.
- **Sonnet 116** line by line: "the star to every wandering bark, / Whose worth's unknown, although his height be taken" — bark = ship; height taken = measuring altitude for latitude. The GIS talk in one couplet.
