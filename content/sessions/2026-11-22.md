A deep lesson on Dostoevsky's 1864 novella: [Notes from Underground: Dostoevsky's Man Who Refused to Be a Piano Key](/topics/121-notes-from-underground/). The sick, spiteful, too-conscious narrator; his argument that people will choose against their own advantage to prove they are "a man and not a piano-key," and that "twice two makes five is sometimes a very charming thing too"; the Crystal Palace of 1851 and Chernyshevsky's rational utopia; the officer who moved him aside in a billiard tavern, Zverkov's dinner, and Liza's five-rouble note. With Socratic questions along the way, and cross-references to Kafka, Chekhov, Hamlet, and Dantès with the [Abbé Faria](/topics/44-the-real-abbe-faria/): the cell as school, the underground as trap. It also links back to the [Kahneman thread](/topics/85-kahneman-tversky-the-first-evening/). [Lesson PDF](/downloads/Notes_from_Underground_Dostoevsky_1864_Faria_Lesson_2026-11-22.pdf).
