Two sections from the same day. First, a set of lessons on the word-type terms that are easy to mix up: acronym, homonym, and the rest. The hub is [Confusing Word Terms: Acronym, Homonym, and the Rest](/topics/133-confusing-word-terms/). Four grouped pages follow: [Shortened Words](/topics/134-shortened-words-abbreviation-to-backronym/) (abbreviation, contraction, acronym, initialism, backronym, and the myths that *posh*, *SOS*, and *golf* are acronyms); [Same Sound, Same Spelling, or Neither](/topics/135-homonym-homophone-homograph-heteronym/) (homonym, homophone, homograph, heteronym, and capitonym, with a chart of sound by spelling); [The -nym Family](/topics/136-nym-family-synonym-to-retronym/) (synonym, antonym, contronym, eponym, toponym, demonym, and retronym); and [Wordplay and Slips](/topics/137-wordplay-and-slips/) (portmanteau, onomatopoeia, palindrome, anagram, malapropism, eggcorn, mondegreen, spoonerism, oxymoron, euphemism, and neologism). A desk sheet covers the whole set: [Confusing Word Terms (PDF)](/downloads/Confusing_Word_Terms_Acronym_to_Eggcorn_Faria_2026-11-26.pdf). Then a refresher that puts classic fallacies beside the Kahneman and Tversky findings. The hub is [Logical Fallacies and Kahneman-Tversky Biases](/topics/138-logical-fallacies-and-kahneman-tversky-biases/): a fallacy is a move in an argument, a bias is a tilt in the mind, with a chart of the closest cousins and the papers in order, from the 1974 *Science* article to *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011). Then [Fourteen Classic Fallacies](/topics/139-fourteen-classic-fallacies/): straw man, ad hominem, appeal to authority, false dilemma, slippery slope, begging the question, post hoc, hasty generalization, red herring, tu quoque, no true Scotsman, bandwagon, appeal to nature, and the Texas sharpshooter. Then [Mental Shortcuts: System 1, WYSIATI, and the Three Heuristics](/topics/140-mental-shortcuts-system-1-wysiati-and-the-three-heuristics/): how the two systems were named, what "what you see is all there is" means, and anchoring, availability, and representativeness. Then [Probability Traps: Linda, Base Rates, and Regression to the Mean](/topics/141-probability-traps-linda-base-rates-and-regression/): the conjunction fallacy, the cab problem, the law of small numbers, the gambler's fallacy versus the hot hand, and the Israeli flight instructors, all in plain counts. Then [Risk, Frames, and Later Judgment](/topics/142-risk-frames-and-later-judgment/): prospect theory, the Asian-disease framing problem, loss aversion, the endowment effect, sunk cost, the planning fallacy, the halo effect, hindsight and outcome bias, and overconfidence. This continues the thread that began in [the note of one evening](/topics/85-kahneman-tversky-the-first-evening/) and continued with [the peak-end rule](/topics/120-peak-end-rule/). The web pages are the lessons. A printable deep dive is the [refresher PDF](/downloads/Logical_Fallacies_and_Kahneman_Tversky_Biases_Refresher_Faria_2026-11-26.pdf).
